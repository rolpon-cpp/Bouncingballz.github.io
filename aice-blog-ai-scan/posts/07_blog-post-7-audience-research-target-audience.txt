TITLE: Blog Post #7: Audience Research & Target Audience

MODIFIED: 2026-08-31T18:08:21

My media product is a Gaming Magazine. I will be covering the development of video games and how modern games are produced. It’s important to understand my audience in order to figure out the best ways to design my graphics and structuring to appeal to my main audience.

For my research, I decided to ask school students at the local eSports club at my school who were playing games. I was able to ask 23 people in total, most of them ranging 14-18 years old. I chose these people mainly because they represent my audience very well. My audience is teens and young adults interested in gaming, so high schoolers who go to the eSports club will naturally have those qualities.

The research was quite informative. The 5 questions I asked them were: “How often do you consume media about gaming, whether it be through videos or articles?”, “Do you actually enjoy video games often?”, “Do you prefer informative media, or flashy stylized media?”, “What are the most popular games you play?”, and “What other hobbies do you enjoy outside of gaming?”. The 3 most important things I found were that: people prefer more informative pieces of content than hyper-stylized ones, many people had general interests in working on computers, art, and music, and people usually prefer lesser-known indie game titles over big studio projects. About 13 of them did mention interest in making in their game projects, as you can see in one of the interviews:

“Me: Okay, the next question is: Do you have any interests outside of gaming? So, like, do you have anything cool you do outside of gaming? Subject: I like to draw a lot. That’s one of my strong suits. I kinda wanna work on game art, I actually drew some of my OCs for a game idea I had in my mind.” (Context: OCs is short for “original characters”).

These findings are important since they show that people prefer more straight-to-the-point magazines, and how game development is a big share of these people’s interests and skill set. This shows how there is a big potential audience who may want to read my magazine, as we can easily imagine how these 13 people could turn into a large group over millions of gamers.

My target audience is going to be aged around 14-18, which includes teens and young adults interested in gaming. Their interests will include gaming, art, music, coding, and production. They will enjoy indie games and be around middle to college level in education. They will enjoy playing games, watching behind-the-scenes footage, idea and design sketching, and the creation of game assets.

I chose this audience because it’s the ideal audience according to my research. Gaming is an activity most commonly practiced by teens and young adults (ages 18-34 make up 38% of all gamers according to inStreamly, with people under 18 also making up 20% according to the same source), so this research shows how there is a large market who would be interested in my game development magazine (13 / 23 potential interests). People who enjoy art, music, or coding will also likely enjoy my magazine.

My audience mainly needs information to learn and be informed. Because they focus on game development, they need info on how the game production pipeline works and how they might fit in this pipeline. My magazine will explain how games are made and each role that participates in the development of a game, offering these people valuable information about how they can use their own skills to support this pipeline or start it themselves. 

I believe Gauntlett’s Identity Theory will help me explain my audience. My audience is interested in creative work, like arts & music. They will naturally want to express themselves by creating creative products like video games. They may use the information I give to base their identity and learning around it, so they can get a better chance for a job at a game studio and be the ideal worker.

My target audience includes people ages 14-18 who are interested in game development with creative talents like art, music, or coding. Probably the most important things I learned are that my magazine needs to prioritize information and usefulness over flashy graphics or cinematics. My magazine needs to help people get the ideal jobs and roles they want, so I need to keep roles as accurate and realistic as possible. This research will allow me to better design my media product by telling me that I need to prioritize information and accuracy. This will allow me to better appeal to my audience and develop a successful media product.
