There are 6 different camera angles and shots commonly used in magazines. They are High Angle, Bird’s Eye View, extreme close ups, Eye level angle, long shots, and medium shots.

High Angle is when the shots are taken above, looking down at a subject. Photographers may choose this angle because they want to make something feel inferior or smaller than something else, as if they are something to be looked down upon. This communicates inferiority and tiny sizes compared to a larger force. It could cause fear or awe in the audience depending on the character or subject the shot is focused on. A magazine may use this as a cover image or to place focus on an object. This may appeal to an audience who like contrasting powers or forces fighting.

Bird’s Eye View are similar to High Angle, but the angle looks straight down more. Photographers may use this angle because they want to give a complete image of a scene or add emphasis on a large object in the frame. It helps communicate the scene’s full context or subject without much cropping. It gives a wide space to show small details on its subjects. A magazine may use this to create a cover image or give full information on a scene or object. This may appeal to an audience who like stylistic, full shots of food and nature.

Extreme close ups are when shots are taken very close to the subject. This helps add extra emphasis on the subject and communicates small details from the subject. This will likely cause fear or surprise in the audience since this shot is usually used to emphasize an important detail or add suspense to the scene. A magazine may use this for cover images or to build certain emotions in an image. This may appeal to a audience who enjoys movies or dramatic photos.

Eye Level Angle is when the shots are taken at eye level, looking straight ahead towards a subject. Photographers may choose this angle because they want to directly address a subject without invoking any specific feelings. This better communicates other objects or characters in the scene without impact from the camera angle. It could cause no specific feeling or observation in the audience depending on the character or subject the shot is focused on. A magazine may use this to give a direct, general idea on an object and instead use the scene to give the shot meaning. This will likely appeal to an audience who like faces or makeup.

Long shots are when shots are taken with a wide field of view with a subject and surrounding environment. Photographers may choose this angle to help communicate the environment around a certain lifestyle or person. This communicates not just the subject, but their world view and ideas. It makes the subject appear small yet significant. This may cause awe in the audience as this angle is commonly paired with vast scenes. It is most effective when you need to add contrast to a small or big subject. Magazines may use this to give contrast between opposing forces or subjects. This appeals to an audience who may enjoy large contrast, and how small forces can still take on large forces.

Medium shots are when shots of a subject are taken waist to head up. Photographers usually choose this to photograph people, as it gives the person full emphasis, makes them look approachable, and provides space for other elements. This helps communicate what the subject looks like clearly while maintaining readability. This usually causes interest in the audience as people are tempted to read the magazine to learn more about the subject. It is most effective when it shows people. Magazines usually use this for cover images or to represent certain people. This may appeal to an audience interested in celebrities and celebrity drama.

Medium, Long, and Eye Level shots help show authority by adding focus on a subject or showing an environment based around a subject. Extreme close ups help build closeness to the audience by giving detailed information about character expressions and thoughts. To attract teens, Medium and Eye Level shots are ideal since they give good emphasis on attractive subjects. On professional magazine covers, Bird’s eye view, Long, and Medium shots dominate. Professionals use these because they often look very visually appealing and provide good emphasis on a subject or environment. For example, a food magazine I found (Fine Cooking), used a Bird’s Eye View shot on food. 

My Photography Plan

For my magazine cover, I plan to use an extreme close up. I will use Eye Level shots, Bird’s Eye View, and High Angle Shots. These are the best choices for my audience since they show the game design process in detail. My audience is mainly comprised of talented teens 14-18, with skills like music, art, and coding. They will naturally want to see the finer details of the game development process, so using close-up shots combined with angles that prioritize readability and looks. This will help me communicate my genre and purpose by making game development an obvious and easily readable element of my magazine.

CCR Connection

Understanding camera shots and angles allows me to better understand how to frame subjects in a photo to communicate a purpose. It allows me to produce photos like other magazines of game design. When I understand camera shots, it makes copying shots and ideas a lot easier. Understanding how camera shots give meaning helps me better curate my images to my audience by giving detailed close-up shots.
