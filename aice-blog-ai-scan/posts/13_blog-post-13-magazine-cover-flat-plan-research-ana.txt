I selected to make a game development magazine. I chose this genre because it is a genre that I am very knowledgeable about and that I am passionate about myself. I make games and work with others in my free time, so designing a magazine about game design helps me understand the game design industry better and share my talent with others. However, I need to attract readers of my genre first in order to share any information at all, and thus I will need to design a flatplan for the main cover. I hope that analyzing flatplans will help me learn how to structure an image to capture attention while also keeping the magazine’s topic clear.

Flat Plan A

Flat Plan A has the masthead and skyline at the top, which help provide even spacing and clearly communicate the magazine brand. The main cover line and subheading help attract readers with a shocking fast or big story, hooking them in. The bubble helps provide extra information to the contents in the magazine and also helps add additional headlines. The main image is the majority section of the magazine; it’s the stylistic image that attracts the eye first and gets readers excited. The coverlines boxes show additional, secondary headlines that are less important. The web address box is there to show to users that the magazine has an online web presence, allowing them to further get invested in the magazine brand. The bottom Date/Issue, Price, and Barcode boxes are there to help readers and shops identify the magazine, as well as to clearly communicate the magazine’s selling price. 

Flat Plan B

Flat Plan B is similar to Flat Plan A, but it has a few key changes that separate it. Just like Flat Plan A, Flat Plan B also has a main image and masthead to help hook readers in and communicate the magazine brand. However, this time, many of the headline boxes and captions are placed on the left and right sides. The feature captions help tell readers what’s in the magazine and what topics are discussed. The small image captions help add more images to the cover, potentially hooking readers even more with related subtopics. These 2 caption types are spread across the sides, making the magazine look appealing and interesting all throughout the cover. 

Flat Plan C

Flat Plan C is nearly identical to Flat Plan A with only a few minor differences. Once again, the masthead, skyline, main image, barcodes, price, issue, and date are visible. The masthead/skyline helps clearly show the brand and give some spacing or headroom, main image draws in attention and emphasis, and barcodes/issue/price help readers make an informed purchase and correctly identify the magazines. This time, however, the date and price are moved to the top to reduce distraction and keep uniformity with magazine conventions. The bubble has been replaced with a Splash box, which does the same thing (help add additional, big headlines) just with a unique shape and different placement to better attract attention. The smaller coverline boxes have been replaced with one big box taking up a large chunk of the right side called the “Sell Lines”. This box serves the same purpose (adding additional less important headlines), but it does so in a more organized, clean way that allows readers to skip over it if they wish.

These three cover flat plans have many similarities and differences. They all usually contain a masthead, main image, and places to add extra headlines or content. However, Flat Plan B lacks a skyline and an area to add a price, date, or issue number. Every flat plan places the headline boxes to different sides of the image. Flat Plan B is the most visually effective, since it allows the subject to be framed in center with other useful and attractive elements placed to the sides. Flat Plan B also best represents the gaming genre since it allows all the different components of the game development process to be included on one page. The design choices of focus on main image and masthead at the top dominate in all 3 of these flat plans.

Overall, Flat Plan B inspired me the most as it shows how I can fit a lot of game development aspects on the front cover. In my own flat plan, I will include the masthead, skyline, main image, feature captions, and small image captions. This research will allow me to learn what works when designing magazines and how to build attractive cover images. I may face challenges like trying to make well designed graphics, visual composition, or lack of variety.
