TITLE: Blog Post #16: Critical Reflection on the Peer Review Process (Magazine Cover)
DATE: 2026-09-28T21:20:58
MODIFIED: 2026-09-28T21:20:58
URL: https://lalitnunna.edublogs.org/2026/09/28/blog-post-16-critical-reflection-on-the-peer-review-process-magazine-cover/

I peer-reviewed my peer’s magazine cover, who was making a women’s sports magazine. The target audience seemed to be comprised of people who enjoy sports and improving equality in sports, like women, transgender people, and minority groups. The magazine had most of the required conventions, like mastheads, headlines, sublines, and a main image. I believe the wording of the headlines was the most effective as they clearly communicated the magazine’s purpose, as well as covering interesting topics. The layout was not close to professional magazine standards as a good majority of the headlines were bunched near the top and lacked visual flare. The main image (a slightly blurred image of a line of athletic women in a sporting court) looked fine but felt weak as it didn’t contain a proper subject, central idea, or clear purpose. The headline text was very good and properly built excitement but the lack of visual appeal or ordered placement makes them feel boring and weak for the target audience. The font was good as it used a stylistic font but failed to use color variety as the only colors I found were white and blue, which are very monotone and standard.

I was able to learn how to avoid common design mistakes and pitfalls by peer-reviewing another’s magazine cover, as well as how to improve my main image. The main mistakes I saw were lack of correct placements, lack of subject in main image, and lack of stylistic appeal. By evaluating my peer’s cover, it better taught me what exactly are the do’s and do not’s of the magazine cover design process. My peer’s main image does use warmer colors and background blur to help make a more readable image, which is something I could learn from to help decrease the sharper colors in my own cover. This activity taught me that magazine conventions are used to help make covers look good, and straying from those conventions must be done carefully and wisely in order to maintain appeal.

I gave my peer some feedback to help them improve their magazine cover. For example, I told them to shorten down their headlines as some of them were too long. I told them to use a subject in their main image. I supported these comments by reviewing the guidelines provided to us on the main feedback sheet, i.e. headlines shouldn’t be more than 4-5 words, main image should be visually interested, etc. I received some feedback on my own magazine cover as well, mainly that my magazine had excessively long headlines. When designing my final cover, I will shorten down my headlines and sublines by a large amount, making each headline/subline a few words long. I will also improve my main image to be less conflicting with my color scheme. These changes will significantly improve the readability of my magazine cover, attracting more of my target audience to read my magazine.

Receiving feedback from others is important as it helps improve and grow our design skills. Peer review helps the designer notice strengths and weaknesses they may have not noticed before by letting someone else with a fresh perspective view the magazine cover without bias. This means that issues, bias, or tiny details that the designer might have glossed over can be picked up by a fresh pair of eyes that don’t have any connection to the cover. By patching up weak points and reviewing feedback, the magazine cover becomes more appealing and structured in general, which will naturally improve appeal to the target audience. The peer review process simulates real magazine via double review. In real magazine creation, a review team will review and edit a magazine to make it suitable for publication, sending the magazine back to the design team if major changes need to be made. Peer review is similar in the sense that another person will review your magazine cover for weaknesses and report suggestions to improve.

This activity developed my ability to understand design choices, critique designs effectively, and audiences. By analyzing my peer’s cover, I was able to go through the image and imagine how they chose to place and structure visual elements. I also learned how to identify weaknesses in a design and how to give effective feedback to improve the design. I also learned how to identify main ideas and audiences to better fit and understand the magazine cover around the target audience.
