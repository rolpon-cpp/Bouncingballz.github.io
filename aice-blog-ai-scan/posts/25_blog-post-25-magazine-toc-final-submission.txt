## TOC

## COVER

Revisions and Improvement

Main revisions I made were to simply just the size and placement of the headlines and sublines in the first and third categories. This readjust makes the headlines feel more professional and ordered. The category ‘Featured Stories’ also got a lot more emphasis thanks to the expanded font and lack of empty space. My peer feedback was the main motivator for this change, as teacher feedback was good. This revisions were necessary to make the TOC look professional and make it look like a final, finished product. These changes made my TOC look like a real magazine TOC and removed dead space. 

Audience Effectiveness

My final TOC appeals to my audience in many ways. For starters, my headlines cover topics that appeal to them. For example, I cover game optimization and game coding, which my audience (being attracted to video games and wanting to make their own games), will want to learn more about. My photography also draws them in, since I display real photos of 3D modeling, coding, and level planning. These photos give them a visual idea of what they should expect to see and makes them excited about the displayed stories. The categories draw them in my clearing organizing the information into bunches, which fits the straight-to-the-point, informative type content my audience enjoys. The typography also appeals to them, since it is a retro font. The retro font is a callback to early video games, and is a common style in video games, letting them connect video games to my magazine. Visual hierarchy also helps, since my content is spaced out and ordered professionally, causing them to feel like the magazine is a professional and serious publication.

Application of Research

Research significantly influenced the final product, as it caused me to make a lot of specific design decisions and choices. TOC Research showed me common TOC magazine conventions and ideas for layouts, which helped me formulate my final layout. Audience research taught me that I needed to have minimalist, straight–to–the–point, informative graphics. Flat plan research showed me how designers come up with good looking layouts and where they start to construct their ideas. Professional magazine research showed me how top and well-known magazines look appealing and stand out from the rest; relying on specific styles, bold imagery, and unique graphical designs. Each research session caused me to develop a vague idea of what the TOC would look like in my head, as well as specific design choices that I would make. 

Evaluation of the Final Product

I think my greatest strengths are my images and colors. The best design choices were make the Featured Stories box the biggest and to keep the main image entirely on the left page. The TOC guides readers thanks to the simple, 3 vertical category layout, which is familiar to readers, but remains unique by going in descending order instead of ascending order. The TOC also fits the magazine genre since the categories and headlines specifically cover game related content. If I had more time, I would obtain better photos and add a visually unique graphic, like the robot punching through the page in the middle of the TOC.

Reflection

I learned how to design appealing TOCs, use colors, layout, and incorporate feedback. My work evolved from planning to final by starting with flat plans and research, moving up to drafts, getting feedback from students and teachers, and then finally doing refinements to the TOC. The drafting stage was the most difficult since I had to make the magazine under a time limit. The most difficult challenge was figuring out how to lay out my elements. This project made me a stronger media studies student by teaching me how to design well and how to work under time crunches.
