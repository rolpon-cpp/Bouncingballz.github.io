## What is a DPS?

A DPS (Double Page Spread) is two or more adjacent pages next to each other that are combined together make a wider area for text and content. In a magazine, a DPS is used to pack content together and make reading content feel more natural. 

## Magazine Conventions

Images

Definition: Images in magazines commonly comprise of screenshots, 2d graphics, cinematic imagery, real life photos, scenery, subjects, and/or animals.
Purpose: Images are used in magazines to help add visual and artistic flare, as well as to give a visual representation of the article and text.
Helps Communicate Info to Readers: This helps readers to better understand and visualize the text, especially if the main subject of the text is difficult to describe in words, or if readers aren’t completely comprehending the text.

Color Scheme

Definition: Color schemes in magazines commonly comprise of standard, complementary colors used to color text boxes, graphics, images, borders, or content dividers.
Purpose: They are used to help give the magazine a consistent theme, make appealing imagery, and sometimes invoke specific emotions.
Helps Communicate Info to Readers: This doesn’t necessary communicate specific information to readers, but it makes the magazine feel visually consistent and professional, causing readers to trust and enjoy the content.

Pull Quote

Definition: A quote from an important person, book, or related piece of content. 
Purpose: To add credibility or a thoughtful comment to a story.
Helps Communicate Info to Readers: Helps add commentary and expectations to the article/text.

Headline

Definition: Big, flashy text boxes/labels to attract attention
Purpose: Draw readers in and make them excited
Helps Communicate Info to Readers: Helps communicate what the magazine is about and the kind of content it contains to readers

Caption

Definition: Small pieces of text on images or headlines
Purpose: To clarify or add additional context to specific information
Helps Communicate Info to Readers: Helps add context to headlines and pictures

Drop Cap

Definition: Enlarged capital letter at the start of a sentence
Purpose: To indicate where the article starts and add visual flare
Helps Communicate Info to Readers: Makes the start of the articles/paragraphs clear.

Fact Box

Definition: Small box containing facts and bits of information
Purpose: To add extra context, excite readers, and provide information quickly
Helps Communicate Info to Readers: Gives fun or important information quickly

Standfirst

Definition: Small, short introductory paragraph summarizing the magazine content right after the headline
Purpose: To hook readers in 
Helps Communicate Info to Readers: To give a quick summary of the article/magazine content, allowing them to get to the information they want quickly

## Audience and Purpose

Magazines use different design styles to appeal to different audiences for a variety of reasons. An audience interested in heavy and realistic video games may like a gritty, dark design while mothers taking care of their babies may like a soft, innocent design. Audiences like different things and have different lives, and it’s important to have a design that compliments and reflects their lifestyle and preferences. Layout and design can impact a reader’s view the magazine and how they interpret the information. A minimalist-feel can help support a professional tone, while a more cluttered, packed look can make the reader feel like they are reading an older, 2000s magazine.

## Reflection

I believe the images, headlines, and captions are the most important conventions. They are effective because they are the biggest, largest, highest-impact graphics elements inside most magazines. They support the whole vibe, role, and impression of the magazine. I will be relying on these conventions in my own magazine DPS as they are common practice and have been proven to work well and effectively.

## Essential Question

How do magazine conventions help communicate information and engage readers?

Magazine conventions help engage readers by using clever tricks and using tried-and-true methods. For example, is a common convention with images to make them graphically distinct with a clever effect or crop, which helps draw attention to the magazine as it is unique and distinct. Headlines are almost always large and made to prioritize the highlights of a story. This tried-and-true method works for attracting readers by hooking them in with the best the magazine has to offer. Magazine conventions also communicate information by packing information into summaries and infographics. Using an article alone is difficult, since readers need to read and understand the full article. By using summaries and infographics, the magazine can communicate that information much quicker and more efficiently.
