## DPS #1 (Texas Law Magazine, Fall 2024 Issue)

## DPS #2 (New Orleans Magazine, “Rise + Shine”)

Publication Info

DPS #1 is from the Texas Law Magazine, while DPS #2 is from the New Orleans Magazine.The articles in DPS #1 discuss student sports, while the article in DPS #2 covers old letters from a city. The target audience for DPS #1 seems to be people interested in laws, sports, AI, and Texas news while the target audience for DPS #2 seems to cover a wider variety of people, specifically those interested in New Orleans’ news and history. DPS #1 seems to be focused on health, policy, and education, while DPS #2 is focused on regional lifestyle. 

Convention Analysis

Both DPSes share 5 conventions: Minimal elements, lots of white space, simple fonts, singular striking images, and enlarged font sizes for headlines, captions, or paragraph separators. Both DPSes intentionally use few visual elements in order to reduce confusion design patterns and support a minimalist feel, which affects the audience by making the magazine feel professional and modern. White space is added to the sides of paragraphs, lines, boxes and more to help clearly separate information and provide natural points for the magazine to fold and be held, which impacts the audience by signaling that their experience and readability have been thought after for. Simple fonts are used to make the information clear, familiar, and easily readable, which gives audiences a sense of familiarity (with other magazines they’ve read) and professionalism. Both DPSes use singular images (excluding the advertisement image in DPS #2), which is done to give more impact and intrigue to each image. This impacts the audience and causes to look and process the images more. Enlarged font sizes are used to help separate paragraphs and sections, which helps organize the information and articles. This helps communicate to the audience that the magazine is professional and well-organized. 

Layout Comparison

Both look very similar, with almost identical typography, color schemes, balance between text and visuals, and reader navigation. However, the fonts are slightly different and DPS #1 contains more colors, helping it look more interesting. DPS #1 uses images to help build modern graphic design elements, while DPS #2 uses images to give impact/idea on what the article topic is focusing on. 

Audience Comparison

DPS #1 appeals to its target audience with quality graphic design elements, relatable stories tied with policy (students), while DPS #2 appeals to its target audience by discussing regional history and lifestyle. The modern graphics design choice in DPS #1 was made to relate to younger generations, who like minimalist and modern designs. DPS #2 uses more simple, bland visual elements and style, which was made to appeal to an older generation/audience, who prefer familiar and clear styles. 

Evaluation

I believe DPS #1 is more effective. The vibrant color choice, modern graphics design, and generally relevant topic makes it more attractive and interesting to read than DPS #2. In my own DPS, I will avoid merging ads with my content, using boring-newspaper like fonts, and having bland topics and content. DPS #2 uses ads and has a boring newspaper-like style, making it look unappealing and unattractive to most. 

Reflection

I learned what DPSes look like and what makes a DPS look game versus something that looks unappealing. I want to use the color scheming, impactful images, and enlarged fonts for my own magazine DPS. This analysis allowed me to learn what is important towards building a DPS and what works vs. what doesn’t work. 

Essential Question: How do different magazines use design conventions to appeal to different audiences and achieve different purposes?

Different magazines use design conventions to help add familiarity & success into their work. By sticking to common conventions, magazines are able to appeal to a wide variety of audiences, and use tried-and-true methods of hooking readers. They can also use conventions to help specifically design their magazines to leave a certain impression on audiences, like bold and unique posing on a fashion magazine going against typical beauty standards, or a travel magazine with submarines or rockets on the cover, exploring new ways and places to travel to.
