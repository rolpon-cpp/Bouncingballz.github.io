## What Is a DPS Flat Plan?

Purpose

A DPS Flat Plan is a design document used to define the layout of a DPS being made. Designers like to create one before production as it allows them to control, test, and construct the layout before actually placing down elements.

Benefits

Flat plans help organize content by showing the designers where and how to place contents of the DPS. Flat plans are also useful to help build and maintain visual hierarchy, as you can see and imagine the general layout of your DPS without visual clutter. Planning image placement becomes easier since you are free to move around other elements and properly plan to fit in images. Creating flow for readers is ideal with a flat plan since you are able to intelligently pick the best placements of text or images to flow nicely. Using flat plans allows you to avoid design and layout problems down the road as well, as you can control the sizes and positioning of everything before you actually start making your DPS, avoid problems with spacing.

## DPS Flat Plan Conventions

- Visual Hierarchy – The way elements are organized on the DPS. This is important as it determines how elements and text will be seen and read on the DPS. It improves the reader’s experience by clearing organizing elements in a way that’s easy to understand to readers.

- Modular Design – A design style where certain elements, conventions, fonts, shapes, and general motifs are reused to build visual elements placed on a grid. This is important as it allows designers to more easily keep a consistent, smooth design style that looks good across different elements. This improves the reader’s experience by making the style of the whole magazine consistent, which leaves an impression of professionalism and makes it easy to read.

- Image Placement – How images are placed and organized on the DPS. This is important as image placement decides how and when readers interpret images. This improves the reader’s experiences by ensuring images are read in the way the author intended, which means readers will understand the article much better and be more immersed in the story.

- Dominant Image – The main, big image that catches attention. This is important as it catches attention towards the magazine and communicates the tone and style of the magazine. This improves the reader’s experience by giving them a big, appealing, eye-catching image to look at and appreciate, while allowing them to judge the style and tone of the magazine before they decide to read it.

- White Space – The empty space between margins, images, paragraphs, and other separation objects. This is important as it helps ensure there is enough space to place and read other elements comfortably. This improves the reader’s experience as it makes it easier to read the DPS and gives them space to look around and wander their eyes.

- Reader Flow – How readers’ eyes move across the page and read the contents of the DPS. This is important as it controls how readers learn and interpret content on the page. This improves the reader’s experience by allowing them to read the DPS in the proper order, keeping textual and visual ideas consistent.

## Dos and Don’ts of DPS Flat Planning

Effective Planning (DO)

- Use a clear visual hierarchy – Improves the design by ensuring elements look like they were placed in a proper, specific order, making content easier to read and more trustable.

- Create a strong focal point – Improves the design by giving the reader a main focal point to look at and understand, adding context to the body paragraphs and providing eye-candy/appeal to the DPS.

- Plan reader navigation – Improves the design by making sure readers look at the context in the proper order you want them to, which allows for better, more concise, and immersive writing.

- Make important content easy to find – Improves the design by making it easy for readers to find what they want quickly, without interference or difficulty.

- Balance text and images – Improves the design by ensuring images aren’t too big or text isn’t too big, which gives balance and equal emphasis on both elements of the DPS.

- Leave sufficient white space – Improves the design by making it easy for reader’s eyes to glance and read the magazine while providing wiggle room for the designer to change or add elements

Poor Planning (DON’T)

- Ignore alignment – Harms the design by making the DPS difficult to read or process, and gives unprofessional suspects in readers

- Overlap elements unnecessarily – Harms the design by making it difficult to look at important images or information, giving the reader a hard time.

- Use too many focal points – Harms the design by making it overstimulating for readers to read and understand, making focal points feel redundant and intrusive. 

- Crowd pages with too much content – Harms the design by making it difficult for both readers and designers to read the page well, and be able to change it freely.

- Create confusing reader pathways – Harms the design by causing readers become confused and annoyed trying to read the body paragraphs.

- Use inconsistent layouts – Harms the design by giving unprofessional energy and makes it difficult for the designer to change elements and keep them consistent. 

## Applying the Research

My DPS Flat Plan Design Rules

- Images and text will be balanced in size and content.

- White space will be used to create boundaries between visual elements, but they should not excessively large.

- Pull quotes will be placed near the top-left.

- Reader navigation and visual hierarchy should flow naturally.

- There should be only one strong focal point.

- Graphics should be around the page to help represent complex topics and add visual appeal.

- Images should match the surrounding body paragraphs.

- Fonts and colors should sometimes change briefly to add emphasis, but never for long.

- Backgrounds should not be dull but have lots of life and patterns behind.

- Elements should be tightly aligned around each other, and be positioned consistently. 

## Reflection

I learned that DPS flat plans are made very carefully to guide reader attention, keep visual appeal, and build a sense of professionalism. Dominant image and modular design conventions see to be the most important, as they help build the focal points of magazines and make content feel purposeful and aligned. Designers should avoid planning mistakes like placing elements in places that don’t make any sense, or cause confusing reading flow. I will use big images, organized columns, and pull quote boxes in my own DPS flat plan. This research helps me create a professional magazine by learning how to effectively position, size, and lay out elements on a DPS to preserve visual appeal and communicate ideas.
